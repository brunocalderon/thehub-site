A logo used to need a large version, a small version and a black one. Today it needs to work as a 16 pixel favicon, a rounded app icon, a line in dark mode, an animation, a stamp and, increasingly, a stitch on a tote bag.

## The new test sheet

Studios have started building test sheets before they present a single concept. Every candidate goes through the same set: tiny sizes, one colour, inverted, cropped into a circle, animated in and out, and printed on rough paper.

Concepts that fail at 16 pixels rarely make it to the client.

## Simpler is not always the answer

The easy response is to strip everything back to a single letter in a single weight. That survives every test and says nothing. The better studios keep one distinctive detail, a cut, a curve or a counter, and make sure that one detail survives at every size.

## Motion is part of the mark

More briefs now ask how the logo moves. A mark that builds from simple shapes is easier to animate than one drawn as a single complex outline. Some studios now sketch the motion first and draw the static mark from it.

## Before you present

- Test every concept at 16 pixels and in one colour.
- Crop it into a circle and a rounded square.
- Decide which single detail must survive, and protect it.

A logo is not finished when it looks good large. It is finished when it still looks like itself everywhere else.
