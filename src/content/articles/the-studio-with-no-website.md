Search for the studio and you will find an address, a phone number and nothing else. No portfolio, no case studies, no social accounts. The three partners in Oslo have run it this way for nine years, and they turn down more work than they take.

## Work that travels on its own

Every new client comes from an old one. Sigrid Lund, one of the partners, says that changes the first meeting: people arrive already trusting them, because someone they trust sent them.

It also means the studio never designs for an audience of other designers. There is no pressure to make work that looks good in a grid of thumbnails.

## What it costs

The partners are open about the downside. Hiring is hard when candidates cannot see the work. Growth is slow, and a quiet year among their clients means a quiet year for them.

They show their work in person instead: a printed book for interviews, and a long lunch for anyone thinking of hiring them.

## Should you copy it

Probably not all of it. But the question behind it is useful: who is your work really for? If the answer is other studios, it might be worth spending less time on the portfolio and more on the client who will send you the next one.
