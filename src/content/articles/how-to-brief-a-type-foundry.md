Custom typefaces used to belong to banks, airlines and car makers. Today mid-sized companies commission them too, often for less than a year of licensing fees for a large family.

## Start with where it will be used

The most useful thing a brief can say is where the type will appear. A face for app interfaces needs different decisions from one for packaging or signage. List the sizes, the screens and the materials.

## Name what you like, and what you do not

Foundries welcome references. Bring three typefaces you admire and three you dislike, and explain why in a sentence each. It tells the designer more than a page of adjectives.

## Be honest about languages

Every extra script adds months. If you publish in two languages today and might add a third later, say so, and plan the extension as a second phase.

## What to include

- Where the type will be used, at what sizes.
- References you like and dislike, with reasons.
- Languages now and later.
- The weights and widths you really need.
- A budget and a date.

A good brief does not design the typeface. It makes sure everyone is drawing the same thing.
