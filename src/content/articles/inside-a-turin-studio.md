The studio sits on the second floor of an old printing works, above a bakery that opens at five. Four people work there. None of them has a title.

## Saying no

The studio takes on six projects a year. It turns down most requests with a short, polite email, and keeps a waiting list for the clients it wants. It does not pitch, and it does not enter competitions.

The founders say the rule is simple: if they would not be proud to show the work in ten years, they do not start it.

## How a project runs

Every project starts with two weeks of research and no drawing at all. The team visits the client, reads everything the company has published, and writes a one page summary in plain language. Only when the client agrees with the summary does anyone open a sketchbook.

## Small on purpose

The team has been offered investment twice and turned it down both times. Growing would mean account managers, sales targets and work they did not choose.

## What we took away

- A short waiting list is better than a long sales pipeline.
- Writing the brief yourself catches problems early.
- Staying small is a decision, not an accident.

The bakery downstairs has had the same logo for forty years. The studio has never been asked to change it, and hopes it never will be.
