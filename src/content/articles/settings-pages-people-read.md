Nobody opens a settings page for fun. They arrive with one question, usually in a hurry, often annoyed. The page has a few seconds to help them before they give up and write to support.

## Group by question, not by system

Most settings pages mirror the database: account, billing, notifications, integrations. People do not think that way. They think "how do I stop these emails" or "who can see my files".

Grouping by the questions people ask makes the page longer, but much easier to scan. Each group gets a plain heading and a sentence that says what it controls.

## Say what will happen

A toggle labelled "Sync" tells people nothing. A toggle labelled "Keep files up to date on all your devices" tells them everything. Write the label as a sentence, and add a line underneath when the consequence is not obvious.

## Make it searchable

Long settings pages need search. Index the labels and the descriptions, and include the words people use, not only the words the team uses.

## Do not hide the dangerous part

Deleting an account or leaving a workspace should be easy to find and hard to do by accident. Put it at the bottom, in its own group, with a clear warning and a second step.

A good settings page does not feel designed. It feels like the answers were already there.
