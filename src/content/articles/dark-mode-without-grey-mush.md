Turn on dark mode in most products and you get the same thing: a near black background, a slightly lighter grey for cards, and a slightly lighter grey for everything else. It is easy on the eyes and hard to read.

## Fewer greys, more contrast

The usual mistake is adding a new grey for every layer. Five shades of near black are impossible to tell apart. Use two or three, and make the steps between them big enough to see.

## Elevation goes up, not down

In light themes, raised surfaces cast shadows. In dark themes, shadows disappear. Show elevation with lighter surfaces instead: the higher the layer, the lighter the grey.

## Keep colour, but calm it

Bright accent colours glow on dark backgrounds. Lower their saturation slightly and check that text on top of them still passes contrast checks.

## A short checklist

- Use no more than three background greys.
- Make raised surfaces lighter, not darker.
- Tone down accent colours by a step.
- Test text contrast on every surface.

Dark mode is not the light theme inverted. It deserves its own set of decisions.
